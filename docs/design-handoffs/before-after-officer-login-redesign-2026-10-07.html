Officer Login Redesign

7 Oct 2026, desktop at 1440px. These are mock-ups made on the running page with the design system's own login card (AuthFormCard, as the SMILE and e-Anudaan portal logins use). Nothing is built until you choose. Then: code, then Figma.

What is wrong today

  • Unbalanced: a 448px form pinned to the left of a 1304px column; two-thirds of the screen is empty.
  • Hand-assembled: a Card and loose fields instead of the estate's login card, so it looks and behaves unlike every portal login (no form-level error, no "Forgot Password?", field icons the portal logins do not use).
  • No sense of where you are signing in: the portal logins show a "Signing Into" strip; this page does not.
  • A wrong password is shown on the User ID field, which tells the reader which half was wrong. The estate's card puts it in one alert above the fields.
Recommended

Option B — split, as the portal logins are

  • Left, in the brand's deepest blue: "Officer Login", the one sentence on who signs in, and the Signing Into strip (Beneficiary Dashboard · Department of Social Justice and Empowerment) — the same parts as the portal login shell, so an officer recognises the estate.
  • Right: the estate's login card — "Log in to your account", User ID, Password with "Forgot Password?", Sign In.
  • The row fills the content width, so the page is balanced; the form keeps a readable 28rem measure.
  • A failed attempt is one alert at the top of the card.
Alternative

Option A — one centred card

  • The same card, centred, with the heading and the sentence inside it, and one quiet line under it: "Not an officer? The dashboard's figures need no sign-in."
  • Quieter and smaller; it leaves the page mostly white and does not echo the portal logins.

One decision either way

  • "Forgot Password?" needs somewhere to go. The design system has the recovery screens the portals use (PortalRecoveryTemplate); in this prototype I would add a recovery page from them. Or leave the link out until officer accounts are real.
