## Job 1 — the four unfixed ramps

### Why they need it

On 2026-08-11 the brand ramps (primary blue/navy, secondary, accent) were regenerated by
`packages/tokens/build/ramp.mjs` from mandated anchors. **The functional and neutral ramps were
not**, so they still carry every defect the original audit found:

| ramp | ΔL\* range | hue drift | what is wrong |
|---|---|---|---|
| `dangerScale` | **1.8**–12.9 | 6° | `400` and `500` are 1.8 L\* apart — one colour wearing two names |
| `warningScale` | 4.0–11.1 | **13°** | `500` is darker *and* duller than `400` — reads muddy |
| `infoScale` | **2.3**–11.6 | 5° | another near-duplicate pair |
| `neutralScale` | **1.3**–15.9 | **22°** | top-heavy and hollow in the middle; hue wanders 242°–264° |

The shape rule the generator enforces: **every step 4–16 L\* apart, monotonic, hue held within
~6°, chroma on a single arc peaking at the anchor.**

### The accessibility payoff — this is the point

The only two AA failures in the entire system are:

```
--sa-on-bg-status-error-bolder     4.40:1   (needs 4.5)
--sa-on-bg-status-warning-bolder   4.46:1   (needs 4.5)
```

Both are in `dangerScale` and `warningScale`. Both are rung **600** with white ink. Regenerating
those two ramps so **rung 600 clears ≥4.55:1 on white** closes them, and the system reaches zero
AA shortfalls. When they clear, **delete them from `KNOWN_BELOW_AA`** in
`packages/tokens/test/on-pair-contrast.test.mjs` — that list may only shrink, and a stale-entry
test will fail until you do.

### How to regenerate

Add entries to `ANCHORS` in `packages/tokens/build/brand-ramps.mjs`, then run
`node build/brand-ramps.mjs`. Each ramp needs `{ anchor, anchorStep?, lightest, darkest }`.

**Pick `anchorStep` by the anchor's LIGHTNESS, not by convention.** This is the rule the last
session learned twice, expensively:

- `#162F6A` is L\* 32 — a shade — so it sits at **600**, not 500. Forced to 500 it pushed rung
  300 into mid-tone territory where the dark ink the ladder pairs with it measured 3.26:1.
- `#FF671F` is L\* 70 — light — so it sits at **400**. Forced to 500 it put rung 600 at L\* 62.6,
  inside the **dead zone (roughly L\* 59–66)** where a fill is too dark for dark ink and too light
  for white and *neither* reaches 4.5:1.

**Search the parameter space rather than guessing.** The last session solved each ramp with a
sweep over `lightest` × `darkest` (and `anchorStep`), keeping configurations that satisfy every
constraint at once, then picking the most even. Do the same. Constraints to satisfy:

```
rung 600 vs #ffffff        >= 4.55     (the `bolder` fill carries white ink)
rung 800 vs #ffffff        >= 7.0      (`boldest`)
rung 300 vs #1f2428        >= 4.55     (the `bold` fill carries dark ink)
min ΔL* >= 4,  max ΔL* <= 16,  monotonic
step 950 lands ~L* 18–35    (a near-black SHADE, not black — UX4G's 950s sit there)
```

Keep each ramp's existing 500 hue family. Do **not** move `danger` away from red or `warning`
away from amber — the hue-separation gate already records `error · secondary` as a known-close
pair, and moving hues will trip it.

### The neutral ramp — and the tint question

**You are not choosing whether to tint. The neutral is already tinted** and has been all along:

```
neutral/400  #9aa3af  C=0.0203  H=256
neutral/500  #6c757d  C=0.0165  H=245     <- chroma DROPS, hue jumps 11°
neutral/950  #0a0d13  C=0.0137  H=264
```

Chroma is 0.003–0.020 and hue wanders **22°**. That wander is the defect — not the tint.

**The right way to do it:**

1. **Lock the hue** to the brand's primary hue, constant across the whole ramp. Blue's primary is
   ~255°, navy's ~264°. One hue per brand, no drift.
2. **Chroma follows a single arc that reaches zero at both ends.** Peak around **0.012–0.018** in
   the mid-tones (L\* 50–75), tapering to 0 at the extremes. Above ~0.02 a "grey" stops reading as
   grey and starts reading as a colour.
3. **`0` and `1000` stay exactly `#ffffff` and `#000000`.** Pure white and pure black are
   achromatic *by definition* — that is the whole reason they live only on the neutral ramp and
   not on any chromatic one. Do not tint them.
4. **Fix the distribution while you are there.** Currently four steps sit inside the lightest 7.7
   L\* points and then the ramp crosses the mid-range in two jumps of 15.2 and 15.5. Even it out —
   this is why there is exactly one grey between a light surface and a mid grey, and why
   components reach for one-off hex.

A tinted neutral is standard practice (Material 3 derives neutral from the source colour; Radix
ships tinted grays; Tailwind's `slate`/`zinc`/`stone` are the same idea). It makes greys feel part
of the brand instead of dead. The failure mode is not the tint — it is an *inconsistent* tint,
which is exactly what is there now.

**Exception — a DBIM mode must NOT use a tinted neutral.** DBIM publishes three pure greys
(`#C6C6C6`, `#8E8E8E`, `#606060`, all R=G=B, chroma exactly 0). See Job 2.

## Job 2 — DBIM colour modes under demo tools

### What exists already

- `packages/tokens/reference/dbim-palette.json` — **the complete DBIM colour system**, transcribed
  from the DBIM ToolKit Colours chapter: all six primary groups × five shades, and the full
  functional palette. Use this file; do not re-fetch the page (it is a JS-rendered SPA behind a
  login and will not load).
- A `dbim` brand already exists on the `data-brand` axis, carrying DBIM's **Blue** group with all
  five published shades pinned at rungs 800/600/400/200/100 and the gaps interpolated.
- `CLAUDE.md` carries the standing rule: **`dbim` is code-only and never goes to Figma unless
  explicitly asked.** The Figma exporter enforces this by construction — its Palette modes are a
  hardcoded `["Blue", "Navy"]` and it reads only `colorModes.navy`.

### What to build

**A DBIM section in the DemoDock's `Colour` tab**, separate from the estate's own brand switcher.

`DemoDock` is mounted once, in `apps/hub/src/app/layout.tsx` via `ConditionalDemoDock`
(`apps/hub/src/components/conditional-demo-dock.tsx`). Its Colour tab renders `ColorModeSwitcher`
from `@mosje/design-system`. See `.claude/rules/portal-appswitcher.md` — do not add a second dock
or a per-portal mount.

The section should:

1. Sit under its own heading — "DBIM conformance (demo only)" or similar — visually distinct from
   the estate brands, so nobody mistakes it for a shipping option.
2. Offer **all six DBIM primary groups** as selectable modes, not just Blue: burgundy, purple,
   blue, green, chromeYellow, cinnamonRed. DBIM's rule is that an organisation picks *one* group;
   being able to preview each is the point of a demo tool.
3. State plainly, in the UI, that these are for evaluating DBIM conformance and are not in the
   Figma library.

### The thing that will bite you

**A DBIM mode that repaints only the primary ramp is not DBIM-conformant.** DBIM's *functional*
palette differs from the estate's on every status colour:

| role | DBIM | this estate |
|---|---|---|
| success | Liberty Green `#198754` | `#046A38` |
| warning | Mustard Yellow `#FFC107` | `#8c571f` |
| error | Coral Red `#DC3545` | `#b8382f` |
| info / link | Blue `#0D6EFD` | `#1558b0` |
| body text on light | Deep Earthy Brown `#150202` | `#1f2428` |
| greys | `#C6C6C6` `#8E8E8E` `#606060`, **pure** | tinted, 11 steps |

So each DBIM mode needs `colorModes.<id>` overrides for the **status and neutral** families too,
not just `primaryRamp`. Decide deliberately whether a DBIM mode is "DBIM primary group only" or
"full DBIM conformance" — and say which in the UI, because they are very different claims.

Also note: `#1D0A69` Deep Blue is reserved for the **Gov.In root site** and must not appear in a
departmental palette.

### Accessibility still applies

Every DBIM mode must pass the same gates as the estate brands. The hue-separation gate now
**discovers brands from the stylesheet** rather than a hardcoded list, so new modes are swept
automatically — but that also means a badly-chosen DBIM group will fail the build. That is
correct: DBIM's own rule 4 is "colour usage should ensure accessibility of digital platform."
Some DBIM groups may not clear AA at every rung; if so, report it rather than widening a
threshold, and put it on the separation ledger with the measurement.
