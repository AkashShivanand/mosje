Who Maintains This Website

Maintaining dosje.gov.in needs fourteen kinds of login across thirty-nine assignments. Twelve of those fourteen have never been given to anyone — and every defect in this report sits in one of the twelve.

Section 00

The answer, in one page

A single WordPress installation carrying 146 distinct things that somebody has to keep true.

Ninety pages, twenty-one organisation page-trees, ten division link groups, fourteen telephone directories, twelve record collections and nineteen home-page components. Between them they hold 5,960 documents, 448 officer records, 615 events, 589 gallery items, 312 tenders, 165 vacancies and 140 schemes.

14Roles neededKinds of login required to maintain the site as it stands, across 39 assignments.
2Roles identifiedThe model described on the review call — organisation editors, and one administrator.
12Roles unassigned26 of the 39 assignments have no holder. Every finding in Section 06 sits here.
One finding changes the shape of the problem

The concern raised on the review call — that the same contact has to be typed in several places — is real, but it is not a limitation of WordPress. The site already holds 448 officer records as a proper content type, and every one of them names the organisation it belongs to. The central model exists; it was never filled in. In a sample of forty records the office telephone is missing on 35%, the address on 48%, and Tenure and Contact (Residence) are empty on all forty. The hand-typed copies exist because the central record was left blank.

The most urgent item is Finding 1: when an editor renames or deletes a page, this installation redirects the old address to a page belonging to a different organisation. It is reproducible today, and it is the strongest reason to settle ownership before more editors are given logins.

Section 01

Method

Everything below was read from the live site on 3 September 2026, not from a screen-share or from memory.

  1. Live traversal. The Department’s own sitemap index — 52 sitemaps, 8,702 published URLs across 14 content types. The primary navigation, footer, Important Links rail and home page were parsed from the live HTML.
  2. Record-level sampling. Forty of the 448 officer records were fetched and their published fields counted, to establish whether the contact model is populated.
  3. Redirect testing. Every page held in our August copy but absent from the live sitemap was requested, and its HTTP status and redirect target recorded — with a control address to prove the mechanism.
  4. Comparison against the SAMAVESH content export of 24 August 2026, to separate what has changed on the live site from what our copy never captured. Section 08.

Section 03

The three classes of owner

Every one of the 146 inventory rows falls into exactly one. The classes come from the site’s own structure, not from an org chart invented for this report.

Class 1 Organisation-owned

An attached body’s whole page tree, its directory, its documents, tenders, RTI and contact pages.

Holder: a nodal officer named by that organisation. Settled on the review call and uncontroversial.

Class 2 Division-owned

A Ministry division’s pages, and its group of links in the Important Links rail.

Holder: the division’s own dealing hand.

Class 3 Common

Home page, navigation, footer, Directory, Contact Us, the aggregate document, tender and vacancy listings, and the statutory pages.

Holder: nobody, today. This is the gap.

The call settled the first class, and also settled the working rule for the third: whoever’s page it is will maintain it. That is right, and it only works if the common pages are assembled from the organisations’ own records rather than typed a second time. Finding 2 shows the condition is not met today.

Section 06

Four findings to settle before more logins are issued

Each is reproducible on the live site as it stands today.

F1 A renamed page sends the citizen to a different organisation

When a page is removed or renamed, this installation resolves the old address by matching the last part of the URL only, and lands on whichever page shares that word.

Old address, requested 3 September 2026Result
/organisation/babu-jagjivan-ram-national-foundation-jrf/contact-us/301 → DWBDNC’s contact page
/organisation/national-backward-classes-…nbcfdc/about-us/301 → DAIC’s About Us
/organisation/success-stories/301 → NSFDC’s Success Stories
/organisation/babu-jagjivan-ram-national-foundation-jrf/404
/organisation/national-institute-of-social-defence/dams/404
Control: /organisation/zzz-not-a-real-org/contact-us/301 → DWBDNC’s contact page
The control proves the mechanism. Any address ending in /contact-us/ under /organisation/ is sent to DWBDNC, whether or not the organisation exists. A citizen following an older link, a search result or a printed circular to BJRNF’s contact page is given DWBDNC’s telephone number and address — under a permanent redirect that browsers and search engines cache.

This is a governance finding as much as a technical one: the trigger is renaming or deleting a page, which is precisely the action about to be delegated to a dozen editors. R3 must own URL changes, and the redirect rule must be corrected before the roles are issued.

Ownership is recorded on every record. The remedy is to populate the record and read every surface from it, not to build a new system. That work belongs to R4, with each R5 and R6 supplying their own body’s data.

F3 Documents, tenders and vacancies have no owner

A document record carries slug, title, url, date and category, where category is one of ten document types: Advices (981), Resources (257), Annual Reports (211), Circulars & Notifications (164), Notice (127), Publications (72), Acts & Rules (71), Forms & Templates (37), MOU (33), POLICY (9). None of them says who owns the record.

Without an owner field a role cannot be scoped to “this organisation’s documents”. Every editor sees all 5,960 or none. Adding an owner field to Documents, Tenders and Vacancies is a precondition for role-based editing — not an improvement to be scheduled afterwards.

F4 Three defects that already have no owner, and show it

  • There is no Accessibility Statement. /accessibility/ returns 404 and the footer does not link one. GIGW 3.0 requires one on every Government of India website. It belongs to R13.
  • Two live addresses are misspelt — …misutilization-of-grands (for Grants) and …by-vuluntary-organisations (for voluntary). Both are the canonical URLs; the correct spellings return 404. They belong to the Grants-in-Aid to NGOs division under R7, and correcting them needs R3, because of Finding 1.
  • Four different links in the Statistics Division rail all read “SECC 2011” — pointing to secc.dord.gov.in, About the Division, List of Research Evaluation Studies, and the Handbook on Social Welfare Statistics. Three of the four labels are wrong. That rail belongs to R7 and R3.
Section 07

What the Department is asked to decide

Three decisions. The rest of this report is evidence for them.

D1 Who holds the eleven common roles?

Covers R2, R3, R4, R8–R14

One officer may hold several of them. What cannot happen is that they stay unassigned, because every finding in Section 06 sits in that class.

D2 Directory and Contact Us — central, or federated?

Recommendation: federated data, central presentation

Each organisation maintains its own officers in the officials records (R5 / R6). /mosje-directory and /contact-us are generated from those records by R4, and are never typed. This is what the review call already leaned to, and Finding 2 shows it is achievable with what is already built.

D3 Who owns the seven thematic portals and the three unlisted trees?

e-Utthaan · e-Anudaan · List of Channelizing Agencies

They are filed as organisations, they are not in the Associated Organisations menu, and no nodal officer has been named for any of them.

Real changes on the live site in those ten days

  • BJRNF’s entire tree was renamed — …-jrf became …-bjrnf across all ten pages. The old landing page now returns 404, and its contact page redirects to DWBDNC (Finding 1).
  • PM-AJAY grew from 7 pages to 22, adding Adarsh Gram reporting, village lists, a map, guidelines, PACC meeting lists and a contact page.
  • A fourteenth directory appeared — /nhaa-directory.
  • Three pages moved into an organisation — Success Stories into NBCFDC (a second copy; NSFDC also has one), Ministry and Scheme wise Financial Summary into e-Utthaan, and SCW gained a Scope page.
  • Two pages were removed — NISD’s /dams/, and NCBC’s /gazette-notifications/, the latter now redirecting to a -2 duplicate of itself.
  • Pages our clone carries that the live site does not — /accessibility/, /search/, /de-addiction-centres/, and the persona and policy pages at the root rather than under /home-page/. Our clone also uses the corrected spellings of the two misspelt URLs, so those two addresses do not match the live site.
Two consequences for our own work

The mirror must be re-pulled with all thirty document sitemaps and the eight uncaptured content types. And our clone’s corrected URLs should be recorded as a deliberate divergence, so that nobody copies them back into the live site and creates a 404.
